The check engine light (an orange engine symbol) is one of the most searched car warnings in Kenya. It can mean something as small as a loose fuel cap or as serious as a misfire that damages the catalytic converter. Here is what it means and what to do.

Steady light or flashing light?

  • Steady: a fault has been recorded. The car is usually safe to drive to a mechanic soon.
  • Flashing: a serious misfire. Slow down and stop driving as soon as it is safe, to avoid damaging the engine and catalytic converter.

The most common causes

  1. Oxygen (O2) sensor worn out, causing high fuel use.
  2. Spark plugs or ignition coils causing misfires (codes P0300 to P0304).
  3. Loose or damaged fuel cap.
  4. Mass air flow (MAF) sensor dirty from a poor air filter.
  5. Catalytic converter blocked or failing (often code P0420).
  6. EGR valve or throttle body clogged with carbon.
  7. Vacuum leaks from cracked hoses.

What to do

  1. Check that the fuel cap is tight.
  2. Have the fault code read with an OBD2 scanner. Many mechanics do this for a small fee.
  3. Send us the code with your chassis number. We will tell you which part is likely needed and the price.
  4. Do not just clear the code. It will come back if the cause is not fixed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I drive with the check engine light on? If it is steady and the car drives normally, yes, for a short time. If it is flashing or the car is losing power, stop and get help.

How much does it cost to fix a check engine light? It depends on the cause. A fuel cap costs very little, an O2 sensor or ignition coil costs a few thousand shillings, and a catalytic converter costs much more.

Will the light go off by itself? Sometimes, after a minor fault such as a loose fuel cap. If it stays on, have the code read.
