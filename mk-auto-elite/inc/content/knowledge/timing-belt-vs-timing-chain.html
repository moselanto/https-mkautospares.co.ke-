The timing belt or chain keeps your engine's top and bottom synchronised. Get this wrong and the engine can suffer serious damage, so it pays to know which one your car has.

The difference

  • Timing belt: a reinforced rubber belt. It is quieter and cheaper, but it wears and must be replaced on a schedule. If it snaps, an interference engine can bend valves and cause expensive damage.
  • Timing chain: a metal chain designed to last much longer, often the life of the engine, though it still needs clean oil and can stretch at very high mileage.

How to know which you have

It depends on your exact engine, not just the model. Check your service book, or send us your chassis or engine code and we will tell you.

When to replace a timing belt

Manufacturers publish a replacement interval for belts. Always follow your manufacturer's schedule, or ask us with your engine code and we will confirm it. It is good practice to replace the water pump and tensioner at the same time if recommended, since the labour overlaps.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if my timing belt breaks? On many engines, serious internal damage. That is why the schedule matters.

Is a chain maintenance-free? No. It needs clean oil and can wear at high mileage.
