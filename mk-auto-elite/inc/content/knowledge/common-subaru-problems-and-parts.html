Subarus are loved in Kenya for their all-wheel-drive grip and strong boxer engines. Like any car with mileage, they have a few known weak points. Here are the common ones and the parts that address them.

Engine care

Older boxer engines can develop oil seepage and, on some units, higher oil consumption. Keep to clean oil and the correct grade, and check levels regularly. Gaskets and seals are available when a repair is needed.

Transmission

Many later Subarus use a CVT. These are reliable when the fluid is kept fresh at the recommended intervals, so do not skip transmission servicing.

Suspension and AWD components

The all-wheel-drive system and firmer suspension mean shocks, bushes, and driveline components wear over time on rough roads. Knocking, vibration, or a harsh ride are the usual signs.

Brakes and cooling

Front brakes and cooling parts follow the same wear pattern as other cars. Address brake noise early, and keep the radiator, thermostat, and coolant in good order to avoid overheating.
