Most modern Japanese cars in Kenya, including the Toyota Fielder, Axio, Premio, Allion, Nissan Note, X-Trail and Honda Fit, use a CVT (continuously variable transmission). CVTs are smooth and economical, but they need the right care. Here is how to spot problems early and avoid an expensive gearbox replacement.

Signs of CVT trouble

  • A whining or humming noise that rises with speed.
  • The engine revving high but the car not speeding up (slipping).
  • Shuddering or jerking when moving off.
  • A delay when shifting into Drive or Reverse.
  • A flashing "CVT" or check engine light.

Common causes

  1. Old or wrong CVT fluid. This is the number one cause. CVTs need their own specific fluid; ordinary ATF damages them.
  2. Low fluid from a leaking pan gasket or seal.
  3. Overheating in heavy traffic or when towing.
  4. Worn belt or pulleys in high-mileage gearboxes.
  5. Faulty sensors or solenoids, which can often be replaced without opening the gearbox.

How to look after a CVT

  • Change the CVT fluid about every 40,000 to 60,000 km, or sooner in heavy traffic.
  • Use only the fluid your manufacturer specifies (Toyota, Nissan and Honda each have their own).
  • Avoid hard acceleration from a standstill and do not tow beyond the limit.
  • Fix leaks as soon as you see them.

Repair or replace?

Many faults are fixed with a fluid change, a sensor or a valve body. If the belt or pulleys are worn, a tested ex-Japan gearbox is usually much cheaper than a new one. Read ex-Japan engines and gearboxes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use normal ATF in a CVT? No. CVTs need a specific CVT fluid. The wrong fluid causes slipping and can destroy the gearbox.
