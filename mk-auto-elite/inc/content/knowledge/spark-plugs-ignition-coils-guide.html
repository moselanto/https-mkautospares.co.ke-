Spark plugs and ignition coils are small parts with a big effect on fuel economy, power and smooth running. Worn plugs are one of the most common reasons for misfires and the check engine light in Kenya. Here is what to know.

Signs of worn plugs or a failing coil

  • Rough or shaky idling.
  • Hard starting, especially in the morning.
  • Jerking or hesitation when accelerating.
  • Higher fuel consumption.
  • Check engine light, often with misfire codes P0300 to P0304.

When to replace spark plugs

  • Copper plugs: about every 20,000 km.
  • Platinum plugs: about every 40,000 to 60,000 km.
  • Iridium plugs: about every 60,000 to 100,000 km. Most modern Toyota, Nissan, Honda and Mazda engines use these.

Always follow your owner manual.

Choosing the right plugs

Use the exact type and heat range your engine needs. Genuine Denso and NGK plugs are the most common original fit for Japanese cars. Beware of cheap copies with blurred printing.

Ignition coils

Most modern engines have one coil per cylinder. If one cylinder misfires, swap its coil with another cylinder: if the misfire moves, the coil is faulty. Replace only the faulty coil, unless several are old and weak.

Frequently asked questions

Should I replace all spark plugs at once? Yes. Always replace the full set so every cylinder fires evenly.

How much do spark plugs cost in Kenya? A set of four iridium plugs for a common car usually costs KSh 4,000 to 8,000 depending on brand.

Can bad plugs damage my engine? Yes. Long-term misfiring can damage the catalytic converter and increase engine wear.
