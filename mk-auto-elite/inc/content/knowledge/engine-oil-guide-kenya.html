The right engine oil protects your engine, improves economy, and extends service life. Here is how to choose without the jargon.

Read the grade (for example 5W-30)

The first number plus the letter W describes cold-flow performance; the second number describes thickness at operating temperature. Kenya's climate is warm, but you should always use the grade your manufacturer specifies, because it is engineered for your engine.

Mineral, semi-synthetic, or full synthetic

  • Full synthetic offers the best protection and longer intervals, ideal for modern and turbocharged engines.
  • Semi-synthetic is a balanced, budget-friendly option for many older cars.
  • Mineral is the cheapest and can suit some older, high-mileage engines.

Match the oil to how you drive

Dusty roads and stop-start city traffic are hard on oil. Stick to your service interval, or shorten it slightly for very dusty conditions, and always change the oil filter with the oil.

Frequently asked questions

Can I switch from mineral to synthetic? Usually yes, but ask us first about high-mileage engines.

How often should I change my oil? Follow your manufacturer's interval. Dusty or short-trip driving may call for more frequent changes.
