Kirinyaga Road in Nairobi is Kenya's best-known street for car spare parts. Hundreds of shops sell everything from oil filters to complete ex-Japan engines. It is also where many buyers get the wrong part, a fake, or an unfair price. This guide shows you how to buy there with confidence.

Before you go

  • Have your chassis number. It is on your logbook and on a plate in the engine bay. It identifies the exact model, engine and year.
  • Take the old part or a clear photo, including any numbers printed on it.
  • Know which grade you want: genuine, quality aftermarket or ex-Japan.

How to spot fake parts

  • Poor printing, spelling mistakes or blurred logos on the box.
  • A price far below every other shop.
  • Parts that feel lighter than the original or have rough finishing.
  • No part number, or a number that does not match your car.

Our guide to identifying genuine Toyota parts covers this in detail.

Protect yourself when you pay

  • Pay to the shop's own Paybill or Till number, not a personal number.
  • Always ask for a receipt that lists the part and the warranty.
  • Agree on returns before you pay, especially for electrical parts.
  • Avoid street brokers who offer to take you to a cheaper shop. They add their own commission.
