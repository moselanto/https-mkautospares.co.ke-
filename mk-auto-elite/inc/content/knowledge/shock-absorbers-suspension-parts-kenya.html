Shock absorbers do more than smooth the ride; they keep your tyres in contact with the road so the car steers and brakes safely. On Kenyan roads they work hard, so knowing your suspension parts pays off.

What the main parts do

  • Shock absorbers / struts: control bounce and keep the wheels planted.
  • Coil springs: carry the weight of the car and absorb big impacts.
  • Control arms and ball joints: locate the wheels and allow steering movement.
  • Bushes: rubber mounts that cushion and quieten the joints.

When to replace shock absorbers

Replace them if the car bounces after bumps, nose-dives under braking, feels floaty at speed, or if a shock is leaking oil. Worn shocks lengthen stopping distances and wear tyres unevenly.

Ex-Japan or new?

Low-mileage ex-Japan struts and control arms are widely available and good value. Rubber bushes and heavily worn items are often better bought new. We will advise the best mix for your car and budget.

Frequently asked questions

Should shocks be replaced in pairs? Yes, replacing both on an axle keeps handling even.

Will new suspension fix uneven tyre wear? It stops further damage, but badly worn tyres may still need replacing and a wheel alignment.
