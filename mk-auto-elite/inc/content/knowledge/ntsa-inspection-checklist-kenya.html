Commercial vehicles, PSVs, driving school cars and vehicles changing ownership in some cases must pass an NTSA motor vehicle inspection in Kenya. Failing means a second trip, lost time and extra cost. Use this checklist to prepare your car before inspection day.

Before inspection: parts to check

  • Brakes: pads, discs, and the handbrake must hold the car on a slope.
  • Lights: headlights (high and low beam), brake lights, indicators, reverse lights and number plate light all working.
  • Tyres: enough tread, no cuts or bulges, and a good spare.
  • Suspension and steering: no play in the steering, no knocking ball joints or leaking shocks.
  • Windscreen and wipers: no large cracks in the driver's view; wipers and washers working.
  • Exhaust: no leaks or excessive smoke.
  • Seat belts in working order for every seat.
  • Horn, mirrors and speed governor (where required).
  • Safety items: fire extinguisher, reflective triangles and first-aid kit where required.

Documents to carry

Requirements change, so confirm with NTSA before you go. Usually you need the logbook, the owner's ID or KRA PIN, insurance, and the booking confirmation from the eCitizen / NTSA portal.

Common reasons cars fail

  1. Worn brake pads or a weak handbrake.
  2. Faulty lights or indicators.
  3. Worn suspension parts and steering play.
  4. Bald tyres.
  5. Exhaust smoke or leaks.
