Toyotas are popular in Kenya because they are reliable and easy to maintain. Even so, high-mileage examples share a few common issues. Here are the ones we see most, and the parts that fix them.

Suspension and steering wear

On models like the Prado, Harrier, Axio, and Fielder, Kenyan roads wear out shock absorbers, control-arm bushes, and ball joints. Symptoms are knocking over bumps and loose steering. These are straightforward, good-value repairs.

Brake wear

Front brake pads and discs wear with city driving. Address squealing or vibration early to avoid damaging the discs.

Cooling and oil maintenance

Radiators, thermostats, and water pumps deserve attention on older engines to prevent overheating. Regular oil and filter changes keep the engine healthy and, on belt-driven engines, keep to the timing belt schedule.

Filters and service items

Air, oil, fuel, and cabin filters are cheap, high-value maintenance that keep a Toyota running smoothly and are easy to source ex-Japan or new.
