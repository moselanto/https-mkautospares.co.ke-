An overheating engine is one of the most expensive problems to ignore. Nairobi traffic, heat at the coast and long climbs upcountry all push cooling systems hard. Here are the causes, the warning signs and the fixes.

Warning signs

  • The temperature gauge rising above the middle, or a red temperature light.
  • Steam or a sweet smell from under the bonnet.
  • The coolant level dropping, or coloured puddles under the car.
  • The heater blowing cold air when the engine is hot.

Most common causes

  1. Low coolant from a leaking hose, radiator or water pump.
  2. Stuck thermostat that does not open.
  3. Blocked or old radiator, often after years of plain water instead of coolant.
  4. Radiator fan not working: a faulty fan motor, relay or temperature switch. Shows up in traffic.
  5. Failing water pump.
  6. Radiator cap that no longer holds pressure.
  7. Head gasket failure, usually after the car has already overheated.

What to do if your car overheats

  1. Switch off the AC and turn the heater to full hot to pull heat from the engine.
  2. Pull over safely and switch off the engine.
  3. Never open the radiator cap while hot. Wait at least 30 minutes.
  4. Top up with coolant (or water in an emergency) once cool, and get the cause checked.

Prevention

Use proper coolant, not plain water. Flush the system about every two years, and replace the thermostat and radiator cap when you replace the radiator. See our radiator and cooling system guide.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my car overheat only in traffic? Usually the radiator fan is not switching on, or the radiator is partly blocked. At speed, the air flow hides the problem.

Can I use water instead of coolant? Only in an emergency. Water causes rust and scale that block the radiator. Use proper coolant.

How much is a radiator in Kenya? Usually KSh 9,000 to 25,000 for common cars, depending on the model and brand.
