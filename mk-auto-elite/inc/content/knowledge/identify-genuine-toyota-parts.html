Counterfeit parts are common, and a fake filter or brake pad can cost you far more than it saves. Here is how to check that a part is genuine before you fit it.

1. The packaging and label

Genuine parts ship in consistent, well-printed boxes, usually with a genuine-parts label or hologram. Blurry printing, spelling errors, or mismatched fonts are red flags.

2. The part number

Every genuine part carries a manufacturer part number. Cross-check it against your vehicle's details. A missing, ground-off, or non-matching number is a warning sign.

3. Build quality

Genuine parts have clean casting, even finishes, and the correct weight. Rough edges, thin metal, or a part that feels too light often signals a counterfeit.

4. A price that is too good

If a price is dramatically below the market, be suspicious. Genuine parts have a floor cost, and a bargain that seems impossible usually is.

5. Buy from a traceable seller

The simplest protection is buying from a shop that stands behind what it sells and can show you the part number and its source.

Frequently asked questions

Is aftermarket the same as fake? No. Aftermarket is legitimate third-party manufacturing. A counterfeit pretends to be genuine when it is not.
