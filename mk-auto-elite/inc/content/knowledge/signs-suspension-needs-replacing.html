Kenyan roads are hard on suspension. Worn shocks, struts, bushes, and ball joints make a car unsafe and wear other parts faster. Watch for these six signs.

Six warning signs

  • Bouncing after bumps — if the car keeps bouncing after a bump, the shock absorbers are worn.
  • Nose-diving when braking or squatting when accelerating.
  • Uneven or cupped tyre wear — worn suspension lets tyres wear unevenly.
  • Clunking or knocking over bumps — often worn bushes, links, or ball joints.
  • Pulling to one side or a steering wheel that will not centre.
  • Leaking or oily shock absorbers — a visible sign a shock has failed.

Why it matters

Worn suspension increases stopping distance and reduces control. That is a real safety issue, not just comfort. It also accelerates tyre and steering wear, so fixing it early saves money.

What usually gets replaced

Shocks and struts are normally replaced as a pair (both fronts or both rears) for even handling. Bushes, ball joints, and control arms are often done at the same time while the car is apart.

Frequently asked questions

Can I drive with worn shocks? It is risky, because control and braking suffer. Replace them promptly.

Ex-Japan or new? Ex-Japan control arms and struts are common and good value; rubber and friction wear items are often better bought new.
