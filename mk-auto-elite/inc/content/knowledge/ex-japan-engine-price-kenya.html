When an engine or gearbox fails, a used ex-Japan unit is often the most affordable way back on the road. Engines such as the Toyota 1NZ, 2NZ, 1AZ and 2AZ, the Nissan HR15 and MR20, and the Subaru EJ20 are in high demand in Kenya. Here is how to buy one safely.

What affects the price

  • Engine type and size: small 1.3 to 1.5 litre engines cost far less than V6 or turbo engines.
  • Complete or half-cut: a complete engine with sensors, wiring and accessories costs more than a bare long block.
  • Mileage and condition: lower mileage and a clean history cost more and last longer.
  • Warranty: a warranty adds value. Always ask for it in writing.

How to check an ex-Japan engine before you buy

  1. Confirm the engine code matches your car (it is printed on the block and in your chassis details).
  2. Check the oil: it should not be milky or full of metal particles.
  3. Look for cracks, welding or signs of overheating.
  4. Turn the engine by hand: it should turn smoothly.
  5. Ask for a compression test result if available.
  6. Get a receipt that shows the engine number and warranty terms.

After fitting

  • Replace the oil, oil filter, spark plugs and timing belt (if the engine has one) straight away.
  • Fit a new thermostat and coolant.
  • Drive gently for the first 500 km and check for leaks.
