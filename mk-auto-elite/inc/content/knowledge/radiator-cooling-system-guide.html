Overheating is one of the fastest ways to cause serious engine damage. A healthy cooling system keeps temperatures in check, and most failures give warning signs first.

The main parts

  • Radiator: sheds heat from the coolant.
  • Water pump: circulates coolant through the engine.
  • Thermostat: regulates coolant flow and warm-up.
  • Hoses and expansion tank: carry and store coolant.
  • Cooling fan: pulls air through the radiator in slow traffic.

Signs of a cooling problem

  • Temperature gauge climbing higher than normal.
  • Coolant leaks or a low coolant level.
  • Steam or a sweet smell from the engine bay.
  • The cabin heater blowing cold when it should be warm.

Preventing overheating

Keep the coolant topped up with the correct type, replace tired hoses and a weak thermostat before they fail, and have leaks fixed promptly. If the car overheats, stop safely and let it cool rather than risk engine damage.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use water instead of coolant? Only as a short-term emergency measure. Proper coolant protects against corrosion and boiling.

Ex-Japan or new radiator? Good ex-Japan radiators and fans are available; hoses and thermostats are usually best bought new.
