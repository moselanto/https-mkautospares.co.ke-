With fuel prices high in Kenya, every kilometre per litre counts. If your car suddenly uses more fuel than before, the cause is usually a worn part rather than your driving. Here are the most common causes and simple ways to save.

Parts that increase fuel consumption

  1. Dirty air filter. Cheap to replace and often overlooked in dusty conditions.
  2. Worn spark plugs or weak ignition coils.
  3. Faulty oxygen (O2) sensor, which makes the engine run rich.
  4. Dirty mass air flow sensor or throttle body.
  5. Low tyre pressure, which adds rolling resistance.
  6. Sticking brake callipers that drag the wheels.
  7. Old engine oil, or oil that is thicker than the engine needs.
  8. Clogged fuel injectors.

Driving habits that save fuel

  • Accelerate smoothly and avoid hard braking.
  • Keep to a steady speed on the highway.
  • Avoid long idling in traffic where it is safe to switch off.
  • Remove heavy items you do not need from the boot.
  • Use the AC sensibly; at highway speed it is better than open windows.

A quick fuel-saving service

A service with a new air filter, spark plugs, oil and oil filter, plus correct tyre pressures, often brings back normal consumption. See our service schedule and engine oil guide.

Frequently asked questions

Why has my car's fuel consumption suddenly increased? The most common causes are a dirty air filter, worn spark plugs, a faulty O2 sensor or low tyre pressure.

Does using the AC increase fuel consumption? Yes, slightly, especially in town. On the highway the difference is small.

Can a hybrid battery affect fuel consumption? Yes. A weak hybrid battery makes the engine run more and raises consumption.
